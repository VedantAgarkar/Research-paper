                         EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

Research Evidence Hierarchy
**Never allow Level 0–1 evidence to independently establish a research gap.**
Level 5:
Multiple independent studies + realistic datasets + rigorous evaluation

Level 4:
Multiple studies + quantitative evaluation, but limited realism

Level 3:
Single strong study or multiple weaker studies

Level 2:
Author claim / future work / indirect evidence

Level 1:
Inference from methodology

Level 0:
Speculation


CLAIM-EVIDENCE AUDIT

For every major contribution claimed by a paper:

1. Record the author's claim.
2. Locate experimental evidence.
3. Determine whether the evidence supports the claim.
4. Identify conditions under which the claim holds.
5. Identify what the experiment does not establish.
6. Assign evidence strength.

Explicitly flag:

SUPPORTED
PARTIALLY SUPPORTED
WEAKLY SUPPORTED
NOT DEMONSTRATED
CONTRADICTED

Add a "Gap Type" classification

This will stop you from mixing fundamentally different things.

Every candidate gap should be classified as one of:

Scientific gap

Existing knowledge is insufficient.

Methodological gap

Existing methods have a technical weakness.

Dataset gap

Existing datasets don't represent the real problem.

Evaluation gap

Methods aren't being tested under meaningful conditions.

Generalization gap

Models work in one environment but fail elsewhere.

Deployment gap

Research works in experiments but isn't practically deployable.

System gap

Individual components exist, but end-to-end response isn't addressed.

Integration gap

Mostly engineering integration, with limited scientific novelty.

This is very important for your project.

Your alert acknowledgement/escalation system might be a system/deployment contribution, while staircase robustness could potentially be a methodological/evaluation contribution.

Those aren't equivalent.

12. Add a "Research Question Generator"

Don't jump directly from gap → system.

Have it generate multiple competing research questions.

For example:

RQ1

Can temporal movement analysis reduce false positives in staircase fall detection?

RQ2

How robust are vision-based fall detectors to staircase-specific occlusion?

RQ3

Can a lightweight local-processing architecture achieve real-time fall detection while maintaining acceptable detection performance?

RQ4

How does multi-person tracking affect fall detection reliability in shared staircase environments?

Then score them:

RQ	Novelty	Importance	Feasibility	Testability
RQ1	4	5	5	5
RQ2	5	5	4	4
RQ3	3	4	5	5

Now you're choosing the research question, not accidentally choosing a model first.

14. Add an "Unknowns Register"

This is surprisingly valuable.

Your research project then becomes:

Literature → Unknowns → Experiments → Evidence

That's a very clean scientific workflow.
