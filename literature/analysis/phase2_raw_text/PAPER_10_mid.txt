Dilated spatial–temporal convolutional auto-encoders for human fall
detection in surveillance videos

Abstract
Although methods based on supervised learning have demonstrated remarkable performance on fall detection, these existing fall detection
algorithms require a substantial quantity of manually labeled training data. In this paper, we combine dilated convolution and LSTM based
on auto-encoder, which can be trained on unlabeled data, further saving time and resources, and a novel fall score is computed based on
the high-quality reconstructed frame to detect falls. Extensive experimental results indicate that the proposed method further boosts the
performance, achieving recognition rate of 97.1%, sensitivity rate of 93.9% and precision rate of 95.1% on the UR dataset.

1. Introduction
Aging population has become a global issue that all gov-
ernments have to deal with. According to the reports of WHO,
there are 727 million people aged 65 in 2020, and the number
will reach over 1.5 billion by 2050 [1]. As the aging population
grows, more elderly will need to be cared for, and many safety
issues of the elderly will be highlighted. Among various issues
that influence the safety of the elderly, falls become one of
the major causes leading to elderly casualties [2]. Falls often
happen unexpectedly and usually consume shorter time from
start to end. For the elderly, failing to timely recognize acci-
dents will result in physical harm and significant psychological
impairment. Especially, more and more elderly begin to live
alone, which will aggravate the serious consequences of falls.
As a result, designing a real-time fall detection system to
timely detect and rescue falling elderly becomes more and
more critical.

in supervised manner, which is consuming time to acquire and

To solve the above problem, we propose a novel unsuper-
vised method based on the dilated convolutional auto-encoder
and LSTM (DCLSTMAE) for recognizing fall events in video
surveillance, aiming to train on unlabeled data. Specifically, we
firstly adopts depth colorization to fill holes in the depth image.
Then, dilated convolution is utilized to expand receptive field
with fewer parameters, and LSTM is incorporated to allow
the model to take advantage of temporal dependency. Finally,
according to the reconstruction error of DCLSTMAE, fall
score can be computed for determining whether or not falls
occur. We summarize the main contributions of this paper as
follows:
• We establish a new end-to-end network DCLSTMAE for
fall detection, which can obtain better spatial–temporal
features and train on unlabeled data for saving amount
of time and resources.
• We propose to utilize hole filling method to enhance
the depth and details on the UR depth images, which is
beneficial for obtaining high-quality reconstructed frames
and can effectively protect personal privacy.
• We design a novel fall score, derived from the DCLST-
MAE’s reconstruction error and find out its relationship
with normal and fall events in a video, which is effective
to detect fall events.
2. Proposed method
In this work, a novel fall detection method based on dilated
convolution is proposed. As depicted in Fig. 1, the structure
of the DCLSTMAE is mainly comprised of a dilated con-
volutional encoder, dilated convolutional LSTM (DCLSTM)
and a decoder. To begin, continuous video frames are fed
into the DCLSTMAE, and dilated convolutional encoder and
DCLSTM can be combined to extract spatial–temporal fea-
tures with behavioral information. Secondly, the decoder in
DCLSTMAE is used to reconstruct continuous original frames.
After optimizing the loss function in training stage, the optimal
DCLSTMAE model can be obtained. Finally, fall score based
on reconstruction error can be computed to discriminate fall

Encoder: The encoder in DCLSTMAE mainly consists of
dilated convolution and pooling. The convolutional encoder of
DCLSTMAE uses dilated convolution to extract features from
the input frames. In particular, the input of DCLSTMAE is
T consecutive frames X1, X2,..., X T . The information in a
filter can be abstracted into a scalar using dilated convolutional
layers to parameterize the number of maps, maps size and ker-
nel size. For each dilated convolutional input X1, X2,..., X T ,
the hidden feature gt of the dilated convolutional layer can be
represented as:
gt=σ(W∗ Xt+ bt) (1)
in which W indicates the weight matrix that can expand the
receptive field,σ is the activation function, bt is the bias, and
the symbol∗ is the dilated convolution.
The feature cube, which is made up of hidden features
produced by the dilated convolutional layers, can be down-
sampled by the pooling layer. Pooling operations, such as
calculating the maximum value among non-overlapping rect-
angular sub areas, can reduce the spatial dimension of the rep-
resentation and the number of parameters. Thus, feature size
and parameters in the fully connected layer can be efficiently
decreased.

3. Experiments
All experiments are implemented in Python using Keras on
a PC with GPU GTX-2080Ti, 11 GB RAM. On public fall
detection datasets, such as the UR dataset [8] and the Thermal
dataset [9], we empirically evaluate our proposed method. In
this section, the UR dataset and Thermal dataset are described
first. Then, to explore the contributions of different com-
ponents, related experiments and analyses are concluded. In
addition, the performance of the proposed method is compared
with the several advanced algorithms.
3.1. Dataset used
UR Dataset – The UR dataset consists of 70 depth videos
and 70 RGB videos collected by Microsoft Kinect cameras at
30 frames per second, as well as corresponding acceleration
data collected by accelerometers. To protect personal privacy,
70 depth videos provided in a 640× 480 resolution, including
40 videos of activities of daily living (ADL) and 30 videos
containing falls, are utilized. ADL videos mainly include
sitting down, walking and lying down in bed, etc. Falls videos
mainly include two fall behaviors that falling from sitting on
the chair and from standing position. Nevertheless, due to
occlusion or scattering, Microsoft Kinect depth sensors may
contain error, which will result in many regions of missing
pixels. For learning effective spatial–temporal features, these
regions of missing pixels are detrimental. As a consequence,
hole filling method is adopted to correct these regions in UR
dataset. Corresponding processed depth frame and original
depth frame are depicted in Fig. 4.
Thermal Dataset – The Thermal dataset consists of 9
ADL videos and 35 videos containing falls obtained by an
Android phone with 640× 480 resolution. These videos have
a frame rate of either 25 or 15 frames per second. The thermal
camera can guarantee individual privacy and identity while
still capturing photographs in low-light situations. As shown
in Fig. 5, some samples of Thermal dataset are given.
